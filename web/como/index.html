ZTL · no entry without a permit

Città Murata · Walled town ZTL

The whole walled town (Duomo, Piazza Cavour, Piazza Volta, San Fedele) plus Piazza Roma and Via Garibaldi is closed to cars 24 hours a day, every day, with cameras at the gates. There is no ticket for visitors. If your hotel is inside, give it your plate: it allows one trip in on arrival day and one on departure day (45 minutes each).

When it's on

Every day, all dayclosed

Staying at a hotel inside

Hotels inside this zone can register your plate with the city, so you can drive to the hotel. Ask them before you arrive.

Official source

City of Como · Official page ↗

“Nella zona a traffico limitato “Città Murata”, dalle ore 00:00 alle ore 24:00, di ogni giorno, è vietata la circolazione (transito e sosta) di tutti i veicoli a motore ad eccezione di quanto previsto al capo II della presente ordinanza.”

In the limited traffic zone «Città Murata», from 00:00 to 24:00 every day, traffic (driving and parking) of all motor vehicles is prohibited, except as provided in chapter II of this ordinance.

  • Hotel guests: give your plate to the hotel inside the ZTL; it reports it to the Local Police and you are cleared afterwards, but only for one trip on check-in day and one on check-out day, 45 minutes each (to load and unload). If the hotel has private parking, access is allowed for the whole stay, within its spaces. The deadline for the hotel's report is set by a City regulation we have not found: ask the hotel.
  • No tourist ticket. A temporary permit for specific, documented needs must be requested online (SPID/CIE, 16 € stamp plus 5–20 € fees) at least 2 working days before and lasts at most 2 days.
  • Cars heading to the «Arena» car park (near Via Indipendenza) may enter when the panel at the ZTL entrance shows free spaces (ordinance 11/2017, art. 9). That the car park is still open is confirmed only by unofficial sites.
  • Motorcycles and mopeds may enter without formalities only to reach the public motorcycle parking inside the ZTL, by the shortest route. Electric and hybrid cars are not exempt. Vehicles over 3.5 t or 7 m need a separate authorisation.
  • Pedestrian areas inside the ZTL: «Alessandro Volta» (Piazza Volta, Piazza Cavour, Piazza Grimoldi, Via Pretorio, Piazza Verdi and others) closed 0–24 even to disabled badge holders; «Duomo» (Piazza Duomo, Via Plinio, part of Via Vittorio Emanuele II) closed 10:30–14:30 and 15:00–6:00. Only some squares are drawn on the map.
  • Perimeter not official: drawn from an OpenStreetMap area (October 2026) along the city walls, checked against the City's street list; near Piazza Verdi and Piazza Volta the edge is approximate.   • Fines follow the Highway Code (art. 7); the ordinance gives no amount. No changes for 2026–2027 found on the City's site.

If you drive in by mistake

The fine

€83–332 per day

  • €58.10 if you pay within 5 days (30% off the minimum), plus notification costs.
  • Since December 2024 a camera-detected entry into the same ZTL is fined once per day (art. 198, para. 2-bis, Italian Highway Code).
  • Living abroad? The fine can arrive up to 360 days later. Rental companies usually add their own fee, often €25–60.
  • No penalty points on your licence.

Questions

When is the Como ZTL active?

Città Murata: The whole walled town (Duomo, Piazza Cavour, Piazza Volta, San Fedele) plus Piazza Roma and Via Garibaldi is closed to cars 24 hours a day, every day, with cameras at the gates. There is no ticket for visitors. If your hotel is inside, give it your plate: it allows one trip in on arrival day and one on departure day (45 minutes each).

How much is the fine for entering the Como ZTL?

€83–332 per entry and day, sent by post to the car's owner: for a rental car, that's you, plus the rental company's fee. €58.10 if you pay within 5 days (30% off the minimum), plus notification costs.

My hotel is inside the Como ZTL. Can I drive there?

Yes, if the hotel registers your plate with the city (Città Murata). Write to the hotel before you arrive.

Can I drive into the pedestrian areas of Como?

No. Cars can't drive into pedestrian areas at any time, not even with a permit or a ticket. Park outside and walk.
