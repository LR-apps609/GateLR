 ZTL Perugia 
 Perugia ZTL map, hours and where to park
 Perugia's restricted hours run the opposite way to most people's expectations. The zone over the walled centre is active from midnight until one in the afternoon on weekdays — the whole morning — and reopens for the afternoon and evening. At weekends and on public holidays it lifts at seven. Visitors who assume Italian centres close in the evening and are open by day have it precisely backwards here, and arrive mid-morning into a live camera gate. Not every gate keeps to that timetable, so the afternoon opening is the main zone's, not the whole city's.

 Limited Traffic Zones in Perugia
 ZTL Perugia
 Mon–Fri 00:00–13:00 · Sat–Sun & holidays 00:00–07:00 · some gates differ 
 Two areas — the walled historic centre and the Corso Garibaldi–Tempio district.
 What is restricted, and when
 There are two areas: the historic centre inside the Etruscan and medieval walls, and the Corso Garibaldi and Tempio district running north from it. On Monday to Friday the restriction covers midnight to 13:00. On Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays it covers midnight to 07:00 only.
 Read that carefully if you are arriving on a weekday. A drive up to the centre at ten in the morning to drop off luggage is inside the restricted window, and the early afternoon is when the main zone lifts. Treat that gap as belonging to the main zone rather than to the whole city, though. Perugia controls a number of separate gates and they do not all share the one timetable — at least one on the northern side is recorded as running in the afternoon and evening instead, which is exactly when you would assume you were clear.
 Hours are set by the Comune and can change. Check the gate you actually intend to use rather than relying on the city-wide gap: the shape of the rule, closed in the morning and open later, is the thing to remember about Perugia, but it is not a guarantee that holds at every entrance.
 Piazza Partigiani and the escalators
 Perugia sits on a ridge, and the city has solved the problem of getting people up it more thoroughly than anywhere else in Italy. The standard approach is the car park at Piazza Partigiani, at the foot of the hill on the southern side. From it, a series of escalators climbs up inside the hill and emerges in Piazza Italia at the top of Corso Vannucci, in the middle of everything.
 It takes a few minutes, it costs nothing beyond the parking, and it removes the ZTL from the question entirely. If you are visiting Perugia for a day, or arriving before your hotel will let you in, this is the answer and there is no real alternative worth considering.
 The minimetrò from Pian di Massiano
 The other option is the minimetrò, a small automated line of single carriages that runs from the large car park at Pian di Massiano, out by the stadium on the edge of the city, up to Pincetto at the eastern end of the centre. Parking out there is cheap and plentiful, the ride takes about ten minutes, and the carriages come every minute or two without a timetable to consult.
 This is the better choice if you are staying several days, or arriving at a busy time when the central car parks fill. It is also the more entertaining arrival: the line climbs on a viaduct with the hill town rising ahead of you.
 What the escalators run through
 The escalators from Piazza Partigiani do not pass through a modern tunnel. They run through the Rocca Paolina — a fortress built in the 1540s by Pope Paul III after Perugia rebelled over a salt tax, deliberately constructed on top of the district belonging to the family who had led the city, with their houses, streets and towers buried inside the foundations rather than demolished.
 The fortress above was pulled down after unification, but the buried quarter survived, and you now ride up through medieval streets that have been underground for nearly five centuries. It is free, it is on the way in, and it is one of the strangest things in any Italian city. Perugia is worth the trip for the escalators alone, which is not a sentence that applies anywhere else.
